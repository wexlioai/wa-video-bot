# Style: Scholarly Editorial

The clarity of a great law review or academic journal: clean grids, footnotes, citation cards, diagrams of
evidence and chains of narration. For analysis, comparison between schools of law, evidence and sources.

## Look
- White or ivory page, a 12-column grid, thin rules, a running head ("Islamic Jurisprudence · Case 12").
- Big serif pull quotes; footnote numbers that link to footnote cards at the bottom.
- Comparison tables for the views of the schools (Hanafi, Maliki, Shafi'i, Hanbali) as clean columns.
- Chains of narration (isnad) and evidence as simple node-and-line diagrams, top to bottom, names in text
  boxes (no portraits).
- Citation cards: source title, author, page or verse reference.

## Palette rules
- `bg` ivory, `bg2` light grey-beige, `ink` near-black, `primary` deep green or oxblood, `accent` gold or
  muted blue for highlights.

## Type
- Playfair Display or Libre Baskerville headlines; Inter or Manrope for body and labels.

## Motion
- Rules draw, columns slide in, table rows reveal one by one; diagram nodes appear top-down as the voice
  names them, lines drawing between them.
- Highlight sweeps on key words; footnote markers pulse once when cited.
