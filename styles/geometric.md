# Style: Geometric and Architecture

The language of Islamic architecture: geometric patterns that construct themselves, arches and domes,
muqarnas and light through lattice screens. For openings, big themes, institutions and transitions.

## Look
- Girih patterns (8-point stars, 10-fold and 6-fold patterns) built from compass-and-ruler construction
  lines, then filled with tile colours.
- Architectural frames: a pointed or horseshoe arch framing the text; a dome silhouette; columns in rhythm;
  a mashrabiya lattice casting patterned light on a wall.
- Deep backgrounds with light falling in (radial gradients), or a calm tiled wall.

## Palette rules
- `bg` deep (night blue, deep green or warm stone), `bg2` slightly lighter, `ink` cream text on deep
  backgrounds (contrast ≥ 7), `primary` turquoise/teal or lapis tiles, `accent` gold.

## Type
- Cinzel or Cormorant Garamond for titles, Inter for labels.

## Motion
- Construction: circles and lines draw, then the star pattern emerges, then colour fills tile by tile
  (stagger from the centre outwards).
- Light sweeps slowly across the lattice; the camera rises through an arch (scale up through the opening)
  as a transition.
- Patterns rotate very slowly (≤ 10° over a scene) or not at all.
